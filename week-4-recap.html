Guest essay

Give the Quarterback His Due

Our league has treated the most important position in football like an afterthought. Next season, that should change.

By the Commissioner

Every August, the twelve of us in a group chat named after a running joke agree, without saying so, to pretend that quarterbacks don't matter. We start exactly one and then wonder why the position feels so dull.

In real football, no player matters more than the quarterback. In our league, a franchise passer drafted at 22 who can play for fifteen years is worth about what a 27-year-old running back is, and the back will be finished in three seasons. In a dynasty league, where the point is to build something that lasts, that is backwards.

The fix is small. Our lineup already has two flex spots. Turn one of them into a superflex, which takes a quarterback, running back, receiver or tight end. Lineups stay the same size, and no roster spots are added. A team that wants to start two quarterbacks can, and a team that would rather start a third receiver can do that too.

Our league is built for it. Twelve teams leave enough NFL starters to go around, which answers the usual objection in bigger leagues. We have eight bench spots and three taxi slots, so nobody has to cut a player they like to carry a second quarterback. We already pay six points for a passing touchdown, so quarterbacks are halfway there.

It would also improve the parts of dynasty we enjoy most. The top quarterback in a rookie class would be worth a real argument on draft day, and a rebuilding team would know where to start.

There are fair objections. Teams built around running backs and receivers will see some of their value move to quarterbacks, and nobody likes watching a roster get repriced. Changing formats in the middle of a season would be unfair, so I'm not proposing that. The change should take effect next season, with enough notice for everyone to adjust.

We have started one quarterback because we always have. I'd put it to a vote.

Game of the week

Ward beat Jordan by 58.28 with the only perfect lineup of the season.

Ward185.92
Jordan127.64

Every starter Ward picked was the right call, and nobody on his bench would have added a point. No other team has set its best possible lineup this season. His 185.92 was the highest score of Week 4 and the second-highest of the season, behind Jeremy's 193.02 in Week 2.

Tetairoa McMillan caught 14 passes for 192 yards and two touchdowns for 45.2 points, the best individual score of the week. Kyle Monangai, projected for 8.3, ran 30 times for 146 yards and two touchdowns, and Sam LaPorta added 22.4.

Our line had Jordan by 25.6. Kyren Williams scored 36.7 for him on 80 rushing yards, ten catches and two touchdowns, and Jordan has lost two straight. Ward lost his first two games and has won the two since.

Week 4, game by game

David and Brandon both won to get to 3-1.

Creger156.82
Raj99.28

Until this week, this paper had ranked Raj first in every edition of the Season Power Rankings. He repaid us with 99.28 points and a 57.54-point loss to a team missing Justin Jefferson, Rico Dowdle, Dallas Goedert, De'Von Achane and De'Zhaun Stribling. Our line had Raj by 39.3.

Raj started Jeremiyah Love for the first time this season, and Love ran 14 times for 63 yards and scored 6.5. Rashee Rice played six snaps and scored 0.0. On Thursday, Raj picked up Tyreek Hill, who does not have an NFL team.

Creger plays this league like Machiavelli. Last October he sold Alvin Kamara to David for three third-round picks, used all three in this year's draft and took Kamara back with pick 57. On Monday night Kamara scored two touchdowns and 22.8 points against Raj, and we have Creger trading him again before Week 8.

On Thursday he cut Emanuel Wilson to sign Austin Ekeler. On Friday he cut Ekeler, and on Saturday he signed Wilson again. Wilson ran for 81 yards and scored two touchdowns, 27.0 points, and Ekeler scored 5.0 as a free agent.

Luke is the only unbeaten team in the league. Tee Higgins caught 11 passes for 157 yards, and nine of Luke's ten starters scored in double figures. The tenth was his kicker.

Kenneth Walker scored 30.9 for Jeremy on 177 rushing yards and two touchdowns. Jeremy's four receivers and his tight end scored 30.1 combined.

Matt A.162.58
Reynolds136.58

Matt A. took Reynolds apart. He was a 17.3-point underdog and won by 26.00, the finest managerial performance this league has seen all season. CeeDee Lamb caught 17 passes for 189 yards and a touchdown for 41.3 points, the second-best individual score of the week.

Faced with a choice between C.J. Stroud and Justin Herbert, he chose Stroud without hesitation. Stroud threw for 347 yards and two touchdowns and outscored Herbert 27.08 to 11.56.

Reynolds lost Saquon Barkley after four snaps and Ja'Marr Chase after 15. He has rostered five quarterbacks this season, and every week one of the quarterbacks on his bench has outscored the one he started. On Wednesday he claimed Kirk Cousins off waivers, and on Sunday he started Kyler Murray, who scored 11.48. Cousins scored 26.6.

Reynolds has already traded his first-, second- and third-round picks in next year's draft, two of them to Ward. He spent this week working the phones, trying to sell every pick he has left over the next five years for a quarterback.
