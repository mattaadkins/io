01Power rankings

02Team by team

Here's what each roster actually looks like heading into Week 1, for better or worse.

#1Reynolds
12-2 in 2025 · B draft

He sold his 2026 capital down to five picks for the third year running, went 12-2 anyway, and still posted the best average value in the entire draft, taking Malik Willis at 35 when the board had him at 14.

Selling picks and winning is supposed to catch up with somebody eventually. It has not caught up with him yet, and nothing about this draft suggests this is the year it starts.

#2Creger
11-3 in 2025 · B+ draft

He sold Alvin Kamara last October for three third-round picks, spent all three in this same draft at 28, 29 and 31, and then drafted Kamara back in the fifth round anyway.

He won eleven games with eight picks, four of them veterans, on a roster built to win now. He also picked up MarShawn Lloyd, one of the players Raj gave up on in 2024 — Raj's castoffs keep finding better homes than Raj found for them.

#3Raj
9-5 in 2025 · A- draft

Two Sleeper titles and the best draft grade in the league were not enough to keep him out of his own draft chat — he opened talks forty-four seconds after the clock started and owned the first overall pick within nine minutes.

He used it on Jeremiyah Love, the top player on the board, then spent his last pick on Jaylen Wright, a player from his own 2024 class he had already given up on once. The empire keeps buying back its own mistakes and keeps winning anyway.

#4Jordan
10-4 in 2025 · C+ draft

He has the second-best record in league history, seven picks, and none wasted on a kicker or a defense — and still reached thirty-three slots for Malachi Fields, a receiver nobody else wanted.

He also took Tank Bigsby, one of Raj's discarded 2024 rookies, and closed the draft with a 33-year-old Odell Beckham. The roster is good; the patience is worse than the record suggests he can afford.

#5Luke
8-6 in 2025 · C+ draft

He has the best regular season record this league has ever produced, and he still does not have a Sleeper ring to show for it.

He traded for Alvin Kamara in August and gave him up sixteen days later — Creger drafted him right back. Five picks were left after he sold his second, third and fourth, and bought Matt's first.

#6David
5-9 in 2025 · C draft

He built five rookies and no veterans, the correct plan for a team that won five games, then reached thirty-two slots for Zachariah Branch anyway, the second-worst value score in the draft.

He also owned Alvin Kamara for four days this August, the middle stop between Creger selling him and Luke giving him back. It is the right philosophy with the wrong execution, the same problem half this league has.

#7Ward
3-11 in 2025 · B- draft

He bought first-round picks off Raj and Reynolds, drafted eight players, and none of them is older than 23 — the youngest, most correctly-built roster in the league for a team that just went 3-11.

He also drafted a quarterback, a defense and a kicker for the third straight year, reached thirty-five slots for Germie Bernard, and posted the worst value score of anybody. The plan is exactly right; the execution is the problem, same as always.

#8Jonathan
8-6 in 2025 · C- draft

He made four picks, fewest in the league, and traded his second to David and his third to Creger just to end up with less than half of everyone else's swings.

He also inherited Matt's old quarterback problem, drafting Jalen Milroe one pick later than Matt did last year, right before Matt cut him. He made the playoffs in 2025 on defense, and this draft did not build on it.

#9Jeremy
6-8 in 2025 · C- draft

He drafted no running back and no quarterback. A kicker and a defense ate two of his five picks, and the three players left include exactly one rookie.

There is no timeline here to evaluate, because there is no plan. A 6-8 team needs a direction, and this draft did not pick one.

#10Matt A.
5-9 in 2025 · D+ draft

He traded his first-round pick to Luke, then spent his best remaining pick on a 25-year-old running back for a team that won five games. Jalen Milroe, whom he drafted and cut last year, went one pick later this year to Jonathan.

He named the team Perpetual Mediocrity in 2024. This draft is the trademark renewal.

#11Malcolm
5-9 in 2025 · D+ draft

He traded a 2027 second for Deebo Samuel, age 30, three days before a rookie draft — the fourth trade of his career in four years — then drafted exactly one rookie in seven picks.

It is the oldest roster in the league at 25.7, on a team that won five games, and it includes a second tight end he did not need. He also took a quarterback in the fourth round, a year after taking one in the first round, which is the only sign of growth on this card.

#12Brandon
2-12 in 2025 · C draft

Every player he took fell to him, the best value score in the draft, on a roster that will need every bit of it. He earned the first overall pick by going 2-12 and sold it nine minutes into the draft, to the guy who has won two of the last three titles.

He also picked up Keaton Mitchell, another name off Raj's 2024 discard pile. The best value score in the room and the worst record in the league is not a combination that fixes itself.
